# Word: grasp (agarrar / compreender)

## Meaning(s)

### As a verb
- To physically grab, seize, or hold onto something firmly with the hand(s). Example context: grasping a rope, a railing, or someone's hand.
- To understand or comprehend an idea, concept, or situation, especially one that is complex or difficult. This is a figurative extension of the physical meaning — you "mentally grab onto" an idea.
- To seize an opportunity eagerly (often "grasp at" or "grasp the opportunity/chance").

### As a noun
- A firm hold or grip with the hand(s). Example: "He loosened his grasp on the rope."
- The extent of someone's reach, power, or control over something. Example: "Victory was within their grasp."
- Understanding or mental grip on a subject. Example: "She has a good grasp of chemistry."

## Usage Examples
- She grasped the ladder tightly as she climbed to the roof.
- It took me a while to grasp the main idea of the lecture.
- He grasped the opportunity to study abroad as soon as it came up.
- The concept of quantum physics is hard to grasp at first.
- Her grasp of French grammar impressed her teachers.

## Synonyms
- grip
- clutch
- seize
- understand
- comprehend

# Word: give up (desistir / abandonar / entregar-se)
## Meaning(s)
### As a phrasal verb
- To stop trying to do something, especially after experiencing difficulty or failure; to quit an effort or attempt. (Ex: desistir de algo)
- To stop having or doing something as a habit, especially something bad for you (like smoking, drinking, eating sugar, etc.); to quit a habit voluntarily.
- To surrender or stop resisting; to hand yourself over to the authorities (e.g., police) or to an enemy.
- To relinquish or hand over something (a possession, right, position, seat) to someone else, often as a sacrifice.
- To stop hoping or believing that something will happen (e.g., "give up hope," "give up on someone").

## Usage Examples
- After failing the exam three times, she finally gave up and switched majors.
- He tried to give up smoking for years before he finally succeeded.
- The fugitive gave himself up to the police after a week on the run.
- Don't give up on your dreams just because things are hard right now.
- She gave up her seat on the train so the elderly woman could sit down.

## Synonyms
- quit
- abandon
- surrender
- relinquish
- stop trying

# Word: weep (chorar / lamentar)

## Meaning(s)

### As a verb
- To cry or shed tears, typically because of sadness, grief, pain, or deep emotion. "Weep" is a more literary or formal word than "cry," often suggesting quiet, prolonged, or deeply emotional crying rather than a sudden outburst.
- To ooze or exude liquid slowly, used of wounds, plants, or certain materials (e.g., a wound that "weeps" fluid, or a tree that "weeps" sap).
- (Figurative/literary) To mourn or lament something, often used poetically to describe sorrow over a situation, loss, or tragedy ("the nation wept for its fallen heroes").

### As a noun
- An act or spell of crying; a period of shedding tears (e.g., "she had a good weep after the funeral"). This usage is more common in informal British English.

## Usage Examples
- She sat alone in her room and wept for hours after hearing the sad news.
- The old willow tree, often called a "weeping willow," seems to bend and weep over the riverbank.
- His wound began to weep slightly, so the nurse changed the bandage.
- The whole country wept when the beloved leader passed away.
- After the breakup, she needed a good weep before she felt better.

## Synonyms
- cry
- sob
- wail
- lament
- whimper
